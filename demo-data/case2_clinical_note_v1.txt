Case 2 — Patient ID: DEMO-CASE2-001 (neonatal, forensic/molecular autopsy)

History: Bi-allelic pathogenic variants in MYBPC3 cause a rare and lethal neonatal form of hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) that often evades detection during routine prenatal screening. A two-month-old infant died from sudden-onset acute heart failure. A full forensic autopsy with detailed histological examination was performed, together with trio-based whole-exome sequencing (WES) on the proband and both parents to identify the genetic etiology.

Postmortem examination revealed severe HCM, an atrial septal defect (ASD), and extensive myocardial necrosis and fibrosis.

Genetic findings: WES identified compound heterozygous pathogenic variants in MYBPC3:
- A known paternal splice-site variant (c.2905+1G>A, exon 27)
- A novel maternal truncating frameshift variant (c.836del; p.Gly279Valfs*21, exon 8)

Both variants are predicted to result in complete loss of protein function. Western blot confirmed near-total absence of functional cMyBP-C protein, consistent with a null phenotype from the compound heterozygous state.

Clinical impression: This "molecular autopsy" established a definitive genetic cause of death (biallelic loss-of-function MYBPC3: c.2905+1G>A classified Pathogenic [PVS1_VeryStrong + PS4_Strong + PM2_Moderate]; c.836del classified Likely Pathogenic [PVS1_VeryStrong + PM2_Moderate]). The diagnosis guided clinical management of the asymptomatic carrier parents, prompting long-term cardiac surveillance and enabling preimplantation genetic testing (PGT) for future family planning.

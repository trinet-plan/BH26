Case 4 — Patient ID: DEMO-CASE4-001 (Japanese patient, real case report)

History: The proband is a 58-year-old Japanese man. He was diagnosed with cardiomegaly at age 22, with no follow-up care conducted at the time. At age 57, he developed lower-leg edema and shortness of breath and was diagnosed with heart failure, treated with medication. Approximately 8 months later, he experienced sudden dizziness and a brief syncopal episode, followed by chest pain and discomfort, prompting an ambulance call. On arrival, he was found to have sustained ventricular tachycardia (222 bpm), which was successfully treated with direct-current cardioversion.

A clinical diagnosis of arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy (ARVC) was reached based on the 2010 revised Task Force criteria: inverted T-waves in precordial leads V1-V4 on ECG; MRI showing normal left ventricular function with mild concentric left ventricular hypertrophy; right ventricular dilatation (end-diastolic volume/BSA of 117.3 mL/m^2, exceeding the major criterion threshold of >=110 mL/m^2); right ventricular ejection fraction reduced to 38.5% (major criterion threshold <=40%); ectopic fat deposition at the epicardium. The patient underwent ICD implantation with careful follow-up.

Family history: The patient's sister suddenly died of cardiomyopathy of unknown cause at age 27; this occurred over 30 years before the report and no medical records were kept, so details of her case are unknown. No other cardiac abnormality or sudden death was reported in the family, although comprehensive cardiac investigation of family members was not conducted. Importantly, the patient's parents were in a consanguineous marriage (his paternal grandfather and maternal grandmother were blood relatives), which is presumed to explain the homozygous presentation of a variant that is otherwise very rare in Japan. Both parents are presumed to be heterozygous obligate carriers. The patient's three daughters (ages 26, 22, and 20 at the time of the report) have no remarkable medical history but are considered obligate carriers and were recommended regular cardiac check-ups.

Genetic findings: Genetic testing with a 174-gene cardiovascular disease panel (TruSight Cardio) identified a homozygous missense variant in DSG2 (NM_001943.5:c.1592T>G, p.Phe531Cys), confirmed by Sanger sequencing. No other variants in genes associated with ARVC were identified. According to ClinVar (accessed April 2022), the interpretation of this variant was "conflicting interpretations of pathogenicity," while HGMD (accessed April 2022) classified it as a "disease-causing mutation." Multiple in-silico tools predicted a deleterious effect. This variant has previously been reported, almost exclusively in a homozygous state, in ARVC patients from Japan, Taiwan, and China, and has been characterized as an East Asian founder variant (shared haplotype in 8/118, i.e. 6.8%, of Chinese ARVC patients). In a Chinese cohort, 19/19 (100%) homozygous carriers were diagnosed with ARVC, versus 8/32 (25%) of heterozygous carriers who showed milder symptoms at older age. Allele frequency is markedly higher in East Asian population databases (including the Japanese ToMMo cohort) than in the overall gnomAD population.

Clinical impression: ARVC, first reported Japanese case with this homozygous founder DSG2 variant, attributed to parental consanguinity.
